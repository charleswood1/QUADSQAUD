A familiar rhythm. A fresh conversation.

Five questions.
Life shared honestly.

Each gathering makes room for everyday life and discovering Jesus. These questions give the conversation direction without turning it into a presentation.

Head. Heart. Hands.

Head: What did you learn from the passage?

Heart: What feelings or questions does it bring up?

Hands: How might you respond this week?

  1. How are you doing?

    Share the highs and lows of your week. Listen to what life is really like for one another.

  2. Where are we going?

    Remember our direction: following Jesus and helping others discover him. Return to Mark 1:17 together.

  3. What are you discovering in the Gospel?

    Share something that stood out in the reading. Explore it through Head, Heart, and Hands.

  4. How are your people?

    How are your friends and family doing? Talk about ways to care for them and pray together.

  5. What is your next step?

    Choose one practical response to what you’ve read. Pray for one another as you put it into practice.

Your first four gatherings

A starting path through Mark.

Begin by reading together. Then read between gatherings and return with something you noticed, a question, or an insight to share.

1

Where are we going?

Read Mark 1 together

Get to know one another, explain the purpose of the group, and practice the five questions. Highlight something that stands out. Before the next gathering, read Mark 2–6.

2

Share what you’re discovering

Discuss Mark 2–6

Return to Mark 1:17, then share insights from the reading. Use Head, Heart, and Hands to explore a passage. Before the next gathering, read Mark 7–11.

3

Listen, reflect, respond

Discuss Mark 7–11

Keep the five-question rhythm. Make room for questions and encourage one another’s next steps. Before the next gathering, read Mark 12–16.

4

Look back—and take a next step

Discuss Mark 12–16

Share what you are discovering about Jesus. Reflect on your response and decide together what to read or explore next. These four gatherings are a beginning, not a graduation.

A starting path, not a race. If the reading feels too much, take smaller sections and add more gatherings. Make room for people who arrive with questions—or without having finished the reading.

Before you begin

Good questions are welcome.

Do I need to know the Bible already?

No. You can begin by reading the passage together. Share what you notice and ask what you don’t understand. The facilitator is a fellow learner, not someone who must have every answer.

Can I join if I’m not sure what I believe?

Yes. The purpose is openly Christian: discovering Jesus in the Gospels and exploring what it means to follow him. You are welcome to bring questions, uncertainty, and a different point of view. Listen respectfully and give one another room to respond honestly.

Does “four” include the facilitator?

Yes: you and three friends. One person helps the group get started and keeps the conversation moving, but everyone is invited to read, listen, and contribute.

Where and how long should we meet?

Choose a setting where you can hear one another and talk comfortably: a home, a coffee shop, or another suitable place. As a starting suggestion, allow 60–90 minutes, then adjust together. Agree on the frequency rather than assuming everyone has the same availability.

What does it cost?

There is no registration fee to use this page or its downloadable guide. Your group makes its own arrangements for meeting costs, such as coffee, food, transport, or printed materials.

What if I don’t have three people to invite?

You can begin a conversation with one friend rather than waiting for a perfect group. Think together about who else might enjoy reading about Jesus. This website currently provides a way to start with people you know; it does not offer automatic group matching.

What if we don’t finish the reading?

Come anyway. Read a shorter section together and talk about it. You can slow the reading plan down and add more gatherings; the goal is discovery and response, not completing an assignment.

What happens after the first four gatherings?

Decide together. You might revisit questions from Mark, read another Gospel, or continue meeting around the five questions. As people grow in following Jesus, encourage them to consider gathering other friends too.

How should we handle personal information?

Agree as a group before sharing personal stories beyond your circle. Ask permission before recording or passing on someone else’s information. Why Quad Squad?

Friendship with a purpose.

We want to help friends discover Jesus in the Gospels, learn to follow him, and help others discover him too.

Our approach is simple: gather a small circle, open the Word, ask honest questions, and make space for prayer and a practical response. We trust the Holy Spirit to work as we listen to Scripture and one another.

The facilitator’s role is to welcome, listen, and guide—not to dominate the conversation. Start small, care for people, and keep coming back to Jesus.
